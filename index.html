I study topics in Applied Microeconomics and Development Economics. I hold a PhD in Policy Analysis from the RAND School of Public Policy.

My primary research focuses on the determinants of Social and Emotional Learning (SEL), examining factors that range from school-based policy to early-life environmental shocks. I study how system-wide SEL interventions embedded in public schools affect both students' social-emotional development and broader school-level outcomes, and how exposure to drought in early childhood, a critical period of development, shapes SEL outcomes later in life. My other work examines the economic and environmental effects of the Belt and Road Initiative in Central Asia, as well as the impact of state-level alcohol prohibition on household behavior and well-being.

During my doctoral studies, I was an Assistant Policy Researcher at the RAND Corporation. I have also worked as a Research Manager at J-PAL, where I managed multiple randomized controlled trials on mental health, labor markets, and political economy.
