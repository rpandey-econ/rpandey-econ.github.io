About

I use applied microeconomic methods to study questions in development economics and political economy of development. I hold a PhD in Policy Analysis from the RAND School of Public Policy.

My current research examines how early-childhood exposure to drought shapes social-emotional learning outcomes later in life, the economic and environmental effects of the Belt and Road Initiative, and how a state-level alcohol prohibition changes household behavior.

During my doctoral studies, I was an Assistant Policy Researcher at the RAND Corporation. I have also worked as a Research Manager at J-PAL, where I managed multiple randomized controlled trials on mental health, labor markets, and political economy.
